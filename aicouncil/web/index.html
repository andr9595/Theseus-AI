Hey. What shall we build today?

Build something, unattended

Three agents work one folder around a shared board — an architect plans and reviews, a developer builds, QA tests — and the board decides who goes next. The first turn is a read-only look at what is already there. Every turn after it runs with its CLI's auto-approve flags, so files change without asking. Pick the folder deliberately.

The only thing in the whole run that comes from you. Say what it is for and what “finished” means — that is what the architect decomposes, and what the council measures itself as done against.
Click a chair to reassign it. Model and reasoning depth are set the same way as anywhere else.

    Seating
    
        
    
          Which CLI holds each seat, and what it is told to be. Pin either and
          the router works around it; leave a seat on Auto and it is
          chosen per run. The same two choices are one click away on any seat
          in the bench above the composer — this is the whole council at
          once. The behaviours themselves are written under Roles.
        
    
        
    

        
    What each CLI is good at
    
        
    
          Half of what the router scores against — the other half is what
          your prompt is about. These are starting positions, not
          measurements: nothing here was benchmarked, and a figure you
          disagree with is meant to be edited. Runs feed back into the choice
          on their own, within bounds.
        
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          Roles
          
        
    
        
    
          What a stage is told to do. Every role is editable, including the
          built-in ones. Editing a built-in overrides its shipped text;
          Restore puts it back.
        
    
        
    
      
    

      
      
    
        
    
          Zero-Touch applies in both modes: it is what hands the CLI its
          auto-approve flags. Council can also be granted that at the approval
          gate, so it writes either way; Chat has no gate, so Zero-Touch is the
          only thing that lets it change files, and without it a chat is
          read-only. Everything below protects whichever one is writing.
